Protect your attention.

Your attention is one of your most valuable learning resources. Learn to recognize distractions, control your environment and create space for meaningful work.

Not everything deserves your attention.

Distraction does not always look like doing something completely unrelated. Sometimes it is constantly checking messages, switching between tasks, opening unnecessary tabs or allowing interruptions to break your concentration. Effective learners learn to recognize these patterns and design their environment accordingly.

Know The Enemy

Where does your attention go?

Understanding the source of a distraction is the first step toward managing it.

01

Phone & Notifications

Frequent notifications can interrupt your concentration and repeatedly pull your attention away from the task.

02

Social Media

Endless feeds and constant updates can consume time that you intended to use for learning.

03

Multitasking

Jumping between several tasks can make it harder to give any one task your full attention.

04

Unplanned Interruptions

Conversations, unnecessary browsing and poorly planned activities can break a productive session.

Build Focus

Make concentration easier.

Focus is not simply about having strong willpower. Your environment can either support your attention or constantly compete for it.

One task at a time

Decide what you are working on before you begin.

Remove unnecessary alerts

Reduce interruptions that do not contribute to your immediate objective.

Prepare your environment

Keep the materials you need nearby and remove unnecessary sources of interruption.

Give yourself breaks

Planned breaks can help you return to your work with renewed attention.

Build digital discipline.

Technology can be an excellent learning tool when you decide how and when you will use it.

🔕

Silence

Reduce unnecessary notifications during focused learning sessions.

🎯

Purpose

Open an app or website because you have a specific reason to use it.

⏳

Boundaries

Decide when entertainment and social browsing fit into your schedule.

Before you start studying...

1

What am I studying?

Define the specific task instead of beginning with a vague intention to "study."

2

What do I need?

Prepare your books, notes, laptop and other materials before beginning.

3

What could interrupt me?

Identify likely distractions and deal with them before they break your concentration.

4

What is my next action?

Start with one clear action instead of waiting for motivation to appear.

Give your attention a direction.

When you control what receives your attention, you give yourself a better opportunity to learn deeply.
